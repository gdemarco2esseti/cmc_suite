| FC | Distribute fixed costs of a support center to served centers on planned (not actual) usage | Input: planned annual operating hours of served centers. | DC | Charge variable cost of a support center on actual driver quantity at the planned (standard) rate | Calc: variable rate = budgeted variable cost / planned total driver hours (262,200/11,350 = 23.10/h); charge = rate x actual hours of each served center. | VAR | Keep spending/usage variances in the center where they arise, never push them to products or other centers | Calc: authorized expense = budgeted fixed + rate x actual volume; spending variance = actual - authorized (per cost element). | STD | Maintain a flexible budget that re-computes allowed spending at actual volume AND actual product mix | Input: standard variable cost per unit by product (materials, labor, support) x actual quantities + fixed budget. | STD | Keep two parallel standards: budgetary (annual, frozen) and updated (kaizen-adjusted) | Budgetary standards = last-month actuals of prior year + planned equipment changes. Variance vs updated standard expected ~0 or slightly positive | Ch.5 (Shionogi) | advanced |
| VAR | Provide variance reporting at high frequency (daily/weekly) per cost center and per work team | Daily: direct labor hours vs budgeted work standard (BWS = hours authorized for actual parts produced); indirect labor as % of BWS; weekly scrap and total expense. Reports must be near real time, not month-end | Ch.4 | advanced |
| VAR | Report purchase price variance only together with total-cost-of-ownership effects | Input: standard vs actual price. Warning: PPV drives bulk buying, marginal suppliers, distant sources; show alongside procurement activity costs | Ch.11 | advanced |
| CLASS | Classify each cost element/activity as short-term fixed (F) or variable (V), and optionally on a 1-5 variability scale | Scale: 1 = adjusts immediately (energy), 2 = weeks/months (shiftable staff), 3 = up to a year (engineers/managers), 4 = several years (plant), 5 = special machine that cannot be sold. | CLASS | Classify resources as flexible (purchased as used) vs committed (capacity acquired in advance) and committed-fixed vs committed-step | Used by ABB and capacity reports: flexible = energy, temps, piece-rate; committed = salaried staff, equipment, facilities, IT | Ch.7, Ch.15 | core |
| CLASS | Flag each activity/cost as inventoriable or non-inventoriable (attribute) for external reporting | Attribute field e.g. capital cost, product improvement, carrying cost are excluded from GAAP product cost; facility-sustaining costs added by accountants outside ABC | Ch.2, Ch.14 | core |
| ALLOC | Assign resource expenses to activities via resource drivers (percent of time surveys, direct measurement, estimates) | Input: GL expense lines (resource pools, e.g. 210 pools in the bank case), activity dictionary, % of time per employee/FTE (survey screen: activities listed, allocations sum to 100%, ignore <5%), metered power/computer/telecom. Output: cost per activity; each resource drivable to many activities. Accuracy "vaguely right", not 6 digits | Ch.6, Ch.12 | core |
| ABC | Build and maintain an activity dictionary with codes, definitions and process hierarchy | Entries 10-30 for strategic models, 100-500+ for operational ABM (159 in Stream, 235 in bank, 700 in telco). Rule: ignore activities using <5% of a resource's time; cover 95-98% of total cost | Ch.6, Ch.8, App | core |
| ABC | Define activity cost drivers of three types: transaction, duration, intensity (direct charging) | Transaction = count (setups, POs); duration = time (setup hours); intensity = actual resources used (job-charged). Choose by accuracy vs measurement cost; use transaction when time per event is similar; direct-charge costly variants | Ch.6 | core |
| ABC | Support weighted index (complexity) drivers to emulate duration/intensity drivers cheaply | Weights e.g. simple=1, medium=3-5, complex=10 per product/customer; index quantity = sum(weight x count). Alternative: split the activity (manual setup vs NC setup) | Ch.6 | advanced |
| ABC | Classify activities in a cost hierarchy: unit, batch, product-sustaining, customer-sustaining, facility/business-sustaining | Rule: use driver at the same level as the activity (setup cost by setups, not machine hours). Output: % of expenses per level; product cost build = unit-level + batch-level + product-sustaining (bill of activities next to bill of materials) | Ch.6, Ch.9 | core |
| ABC | Provide activity attribute fields for filtering and reporting | Attributes: hierarchy level, business process, F/V variability, efficiency rating, value-added code, cost-of-quality class, location, responsible person, inventoriable flag. Report: expenses and % by any attribute | Ch.6, Ch.8 | core |
| ABC | Compute activity cost driver rate and assign activity costs to cost objects | Rate = activity cost / driver quantity (or capacity). Cost object cost = sum(driver qty x rate) over activities (e.g. Product: unit-level 9,040 + batch 4,240 + sustaining 1,500 = 14,780 vs 10,440 traditional). Cost objects: products, services, customers, channels, orders | Ch.6, Ch.9 | core |
| ABC | Merge activities sharing the same driver for product/customer costing while keeping detail for process reporting | Group all activities with same driver into one for costing without loss of accuracy; detailed activities retained for process cost views | Ch.8 n, Ch.6 n | advanced |
| ABC | Let the user trade accuracy vs measurement cost: start simple (30-50 activities, mostly transaction drivers), refine iteratively | Targets accuracy of 5-10% on activity/process cost; add drivers iteratively until diminishing returns | Ch.6, Ch.11 | advanced |
| CAPACITY | Compute activity driver rates on practical capacity, not expected or historical volume | Rate = budgeted resource expense / practical capacity of driver (280,000/5,000 = 56/order, not 70 at 4,000 expected). Rate = efficiency of the resource, stable when volume changes; prevents death spiral | Ch.7 | core |
| CAPACITY | Report cost of unused capacity as a separate line (fundamental equation) | Resources supplied = resources used + unused. Unused budgeted = (practical capacity - budgeted volume) x rate = 1,000 x 56 = 56,000. Never allocate it back to products | Ch.7 | core |
| CAPACITY | Reconcile budgeted, assigned and actual expense into spending variance, unexpected capacity-utilization variance and unused capacity | Example: budget 280,000; actual 273,600 (spending var -6,400 F); assigned = 3,800 x 56 = 212,800; unused budgeted 56,000; unexpected utilization (4,000-3,800) x 56 = 11,200. | CAPACITY | Estimate practical capacity with rules: % of theoretical, peak historical month, engineering study | Examples: 40h week -> 32h (20% breaks), machine 20% downtime; or max monthly orders handled without delays/overtime; plus protective/surge capacity reserve; fix the number and keep it until better evidence | Ch.7 | core |
| CAPACITY | Support two-tier rates for mixed committed and flexible resources | Tier1 = committed budget / practical capacity (200,000/5,000 = 40); tier2 = flexible budget / expected volume (80,000/4,000 = 20); rate 60. Flexible budget at actual volume = 200,000 + 20 x actual; variance split vs actual | Ch.14 | core |
| CAPACITY | Measure capacity at the resource level for fungible resources and derive activity rates from unit times (time-driven) | Cost per capacity hour = resource cost / practical hours (560,000/8,000 = 70/h). | CAPACITY | Treat special capacity situations: lumpy assets, ramp-up years, peak vs slack, quality tiers, premium inputs | Machine ramp-up: use practical capacity as denominator (0.30/unit every year) or lifetime volume; peak capacity cost charged to peak period; vanilla vs high-quality network cost split; $3/barrel sweet-crude premium assigned only to lubricants | Ch.7 | advanced |
| CAPACITY | Assign cost of unused capacity to the manager/segment responsible, as lump-sum sustaining cost (rational customer rule) | Charge to a customer only if its erratic behavior forces reserve capacity; else to product line/division/manager who decided to keep it; never allocate to individual products/units | Ch.7 | advanced |
| CAPACITY | Monitor capacity utilization and create alerts for bottlenecks and permanent changes | Operational system compares demand to practical capacity; if demand exceeds capacity w/o delay, raise capacity estimate; if processes improve, raise capacity/lower unit time and push to ABC | Ch.14 | advanced |
| CAPACITY | Link resource usage model to resource supply decisions (capacity must be actively removed or redeployed to realize savings) | Step1 reduce demand; step2 cut/redeploy supply.
